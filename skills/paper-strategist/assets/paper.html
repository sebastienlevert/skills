Executive decision brief

Recommendation: State the proposed choice and the outcome it enables.

Why now: Name the change or evidence making this timely.

Decision requested: Ask for a specific choice, not a vague endorsement.

1. Context

Orient the reader with the audience, relevant change, constraints, and explicit boundaries. Provide only the background necessary to understand the decision.

2. Problem

Describe observable pain and the current baseline. Separate original evidence from an assumption or an incomplete signal. Avoid presenting a polished narrative as proof.

3. Insight

Explain what changes the way the problem should be approached. Make the causal argument explicit rather than jumping from a list of problems to a preferred solution.

4. Proposal

Describe the mechanism, responsibility boundaries, and alternatives. A diagram should explain a relationship the prose alone leaves difficult to understand.

5. Operating model

Describe the smallest credible path to execution, its responsibilities and dependencies. Include owners or dates only when they are established.

6. Proof and signals

Define what would demonstrate the proposal works, what remains unproven, and which signals would trigger a stop or pivot. Do not invent a metric to make the section appear complete.

7. Risks and open decisions

State the failure modes, mitigations, and assumptions that could overturn the thesis. Keep an unresolved decision visibly different from an accepted commitment.

8. Next steps

Finish with concrete actions and the decision needed from the reader. Remove preview content and verify the paper's facts before sharing it.
